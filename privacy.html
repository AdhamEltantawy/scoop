Scoop privacy

Last updated 17 November 2026

Without an account

Scoop keeps your explored hexes, walks and settings on your device only. Your location and routes are never sent anywhere. Scoop makes two kinds of requests:

  • Map tiles from the map provider you choose (OpenStreetMap or Esri).
  • Neighbourhood totals, to welcome you to the area: how many explorers walk your district, and where other explorers left landmarks nearby. To get them, Scoop sends Scoop's server only the id of the district you're in and the ids of the blocks of about 5 km² around you. It sends no account, device id or location, and Scoop's database doesn't save the request (like any web request, it passes through our hosting provider, Supabase, which keeps short-lived access logs). Scoop never makes this request while you're inside one of your privacy zones.

Walk reminders (Android app)

If you turn on Remind me to start a walk, Scoop asks Android's activity detection (Google Play services) to tell it when you start or stop walking, so it can offer to start a walk. It uses motion sensors, not GPS. Scoop only keeps when you last started walking and when it last reminded you, on your device, and never uploads any of it. Turn it off in Settings at any time.

With a Community account

Signing in is optional. If you do, Scoop stores this in its Supabase database:

  • Your email address (for sign-in codes) and the username, display name and home city you choose.
  • The ids of the street-sized hexagons (about 0.015 km² each) you walked through, when you first and last visited each one, how many walks went through it, and the country, city and district it's in. Visit times are used to work out scores (hexes cool down over time) and to bring your map to your other devices; only you can read them back, and only from your own account.
  • A daily total of the points you earned in each area, for weekly and monthly leaderboards.
  • Walk totals: start and end time, distance and the number of new hexes. Your GPS tracks are never uploaded.
  • Check-ins you make (the hex, the kind, an optional note and an optional photo, re-encoded so it carries no location data), kudos, friend requests, challenges and badges.
  • Landmarks you leave (the hex, the kind, a title, an optional note, and whether newcomers should be pointed to it), and which landmarks you found and when, plus your thumbs up or down on it. Finding one only tells the server you passed that hex; nobody else sees who found or voted on a landmark, the author only sees how many explorers did, when, and the vote totals.
  • Circles you start or join: the circle's name, weekday and time, time zone, meeting point (a hex and an optional name), weekly goal and whether it welcomes guests; who is in it; for each walk, whether you said you're coming, when you checked in at the meeting point, and the distance and number of hexes of your part of the walk; and kudos you give. Circle reminders are scheduled on your phone and never sent from our server.
  • Kudos you give on friends' walks, check-ins and badges; open district challenges you join.
  • Personal invite codes you share (each works for one person for 7 days): when it was made, which welcome landmark it points to, and who used it and when. Wrong codes you try are kept for an hour, to stop guessing.
  • Reports you make: who or which landmark, the reason, your optional note, a copy of the landmark's title and note or the person's names as they were, and what we decided. People you block, and whether you blocked them from a landmark.

Reports and blocks

  • Only the people who run Scoop read reports, by hand. Nobody is told who reported them, or that they were reported. When three people report the same landmark it's hidden until we've looked at it, and its author sees that it's hidden. When three people report the same person, their circles stop being listed to guests and they can't ask to join walks, until we've looked.
  • After looking, we keep it, remove the landmark (or reset a name), or close the account. Closed reports are kept as a record until the account of the person who made them, or the one they're about, is deleted.
  • Blocking someone is never shown to them. Blocking whoever left a landmark doesn't tell you who it was.

Who can see it

  • Your friends can see your profile, which hexes you explored and how often (not when), your walks, check-ins, badges, scores and home district. Friendships last a month unless you both renew.
  • Signed in, you see how warm a district is (the share of its walked hexes someone walked in the last 60 days) and how many people keep it warm, never who. Circles are ranked against other circles by the distance they walked together, never by members' own points.
  • Every signed-in explorer can see your landmarks (where they are, the title and note), but only your friends see that you left them; to everyone else they're from an anonymous explorer. People who haven't signed in only see which hex a landmark is on and its thumbs-up count, never what it is or who left it.
  • Anyone can see how many explorers walked a district in the last 60 days, never who they are.
  • People in a circle with you see your first name, whether you're coming to its walks, when you checked in at its meeting point, and whether you walked at all that week (never when or where). During a circle walk, the people on it see the hexes you walk and your distance, live, until the walk ends; hexes in your privacy zones are never shared. Being in a circle doesn't make you friends.
  • If a circle welcomes guests, people signed in can see that a circle of that size walks in that district on that day and time, and nothing else. A guest the owner says yes to sees the circle's name, meeting point and first names.
  • Everyone else only sees an anonymous row with your score and rank on the leaderboards of the district, city and country you explored in, and on the leaderboard of everyone.
  • Hexes inside your privacy zones (set in the menu, stored only on your device) are never uploaded, and are removed from the server if you add a zone later, along with any check-ins and landmarks there.

Looking up areas

When you're signed in, Scoop finds out which official district, city and country your hexes are in by asking the public Overpass API (OpenStreetMap data) about a point in a hex, rounded to about 10 m, then downloading that district's outline once. Further hexes inside it are matched on your device. Hexes inside privacy zones are never looked up.

Deleting your data

Community → Me → Delete my account permanently removes your account, photos and everything listed above from the server. Your map on the device is not affected; use Clear everything in the menu for that.
